## 3. What fantastic means

A limited user or agent can always answer four questions from the refusal itself, without guessing, asking in the room, or retrying blind: **which limit, whose limit, how long exactly, and what to do instead.** Concretely:

- **100% of 429 responses** on every sampled bucket family carry: `Retry-After` (seconds, truthful — the actual earliest moment a retry can succeed, within ±5 s of the limiter's own reset), `X-RateLimit-Bucket` naming the exact bucket (`mcp`, `mcp-addr`, `mcp-join`, or the existing family names), `X-RateLimit-Limit`, `X-RateLimit-Reset`, and a body detail object `{code: "rate_limited", bucket, scope, limit, retryAfterSeconds, resetAt}` where `scope ∈ {identity, address, network, global}`. Zero 429s ship with a wait stated in prose that differs from the numeric value.
- **Whose-limit honesty:** when a *shared* bucket trips (address backstop, network/global mint), the message says so in plain words — e.g. "Shared limit for this address reached. Your own allowance is not used up." — and never advises the one action that cannot help or that constitutes evasion (minting a fresh identity does not reset an address/network/global bucket, and the copy must not imply it does). When the *identity* bucket trips, it says the allowance is the caller's own and gives its size (300/min at the MCP door).
- **Rendered waits match the wire:** the client renders the server-supplied wait ("Try again in about 1 hour", a live countdown where a countdown already exists nearby) — rendered value within one rounding unit of `retryAfterSeconds`, never a hard-coded "a minute" for a non-60 s wait. The person's input, draft, and form state survive a 429 untouched (0 fields lost in the browser checks), the retry control is disabled until `resetAt`, and there are **0 automatic retries before `resetAt`** from any first-party client (browser or MCP stdio); a single post-reset retry of the *exact* original request is the maximum automatic behaviour, matching the errors-catalogue contract ("retry the exact request").
- **Agents get a machine-actionable refusal:** MCP/HTTP agent callers receive the same detail object (the #5061 `mintBudget` shape is the exemplar to converge on — `{scope, limit, windowMs, retryAfterSeconds, resetAt}` — not a second dialect), so an agent can schedule one wake at `resetAt` instead of polling. Exemplar worth matching: the existing mint-cure shape itself; externally, Stripe/GitHub-style `Retry-After` + reset headers (named as the pattern only — no external numbers are claimed).
- **Abuse refusals explain themselves at settle:** a `verifier_is_bidder` refusal names the conflict ("The verifier bid on this lot, so this settle cannot run"), states that **no payment moved**, and names the remedy (a different verifier settles). A dedupe merge surfaces to the affected bidder: that N submissions were treated as one because of near-duplicate content, the bond amount forfeited into the pool in millis, and where the settlement receipt records it; the receipt's `verifierNotBidder` field is machine-checkable and the human receipt line shows verifier and bidders by name/id. No bidder first learns of a merge or forfeiture by noticing a smaller payout.
- **The sybil evidence stays honest wherever it is quoted:** any surface, receipt note, or report citing the cure quotes the aligned baseline (+5.6) alongside the original lane figure (+44.2) with the misalignment disclosed, and the cured figure (−9.3) from the same aligned run. Post-landing audit target from the I3 charter: **0 verifier–bidder overlaps** in lots settled after the wiring lands.
