# Project Room

## A small product with room to grow

84 unresolved choices. Two research rounds. One staged plan.

Prepared for John Potter | 7 September 2026

**Recommendation:** Build the dependable work loop before the open marketplace: bring a real request into a room, let a person or an authorized agent contribute from their own tools, return a reviewable result, and make the next decision clear. Rewards are optional agreements on that work, not a second product.

The opportunity is not simply more agents, more tasks or more integrations. It is less work for people to establish context, make a fair commitment, check the result and continue. That advantage remains a hypothesis to test against capable existing tools.

This report includes the complete question inventory, provisional answers, evidence limits, owner decisions, and release gates. It is research and planning, not implementation, legal advice, provider approval, demand validation or authorization to spend.

**Owner decision added:** Project Room will collect and distribute regular payments and stablecoins through the existing Stripe relationship. Section 11 separates that confirmed direction from account eligibility, responsibilities and capabilities still to verify.

## 1. The whole-project decision

### Preserve the core; make expansion conditional

Project Room already has the useful beginnings of a shared work system: conversation, invitations, explicit work, exact-result evidence, reviews, owner decisions and a structured HTTP client. The next investment should make this loop easier to enter, leave, resume and use from outside the browser. Replacing it with a large orchestration or marketplace framework would create work before establishing a need.

The product should be useful to one owner and one collaborator before a public community arrives. A room must not require an entire team to migrate from its existing chat or repository.

### A narrower first job to test

**Candidate:** Obtain a bounded, reproducible evaluation or other digital deliverable from someone working in a different environment, then decide what to do with the result.

For example, a small product team needs a documented comparison against an approved baseline. One contributor runs the evaluation or produces the artifact; another checks important evidence where warranted; the owner decides whether the change is useful. Dasha Compute can eventually supply execution, while the room owns the agreement, evidence and decision. Before that integration, the same job can be completed manually or through the existing HTTP client.

This is a proposed starting point, not a proven market. Documentation corrections, bounded research checks and product-feedback batches are adjacent candidates. Choose among them using actual existing work, not invented tasks created merely to demonstrate the app.

### What is not yet distinctive

Linear already documents human ownership with agent delegation. GitHub documents third-party coding agents, and its February 2026 Agent HQ announcement explicitly emphasizes keeping context, history and review across providers. Merely supporting several agents or preserving their messages is not sufficient differentiation. [Linear delegation](https://linear.app/docs/assigning-issues), [GitHub Agent HQ](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/pick-your-agent-use-claude-and-codex-on-agent-hq/)

Our testable advantage would be a lightweight agreement and evidence handoff across people, tools and non-repository work, with proportionate review and clear compensation when needed. Existing tools may still win. That result should cause simplification, not a larger feature list.

### The revised priorities

- **Now:** reliable return/recovery, quiet next actions, selected-context briefs and manual/HTTP result return.
- **Next:** bounded reward agreements, assignment rather than uncontrolled races, clear review timing, scoped contributors and Stripe payment preparation. Regular payments and stablecoins are the confirmed target; live money remains gated.
- **Only when demanded:** one proven MCP integration, recurring expert work, and one authorized Compute adapter.
- **Later, separately gated:** public discovery, pooled funding, work-trade infrastructure, physical tasks, unattended execution and further protocols.

Until then, Room must not promise stopped charges, exact cost or safe automatic resubmission.

## 3. What the two rounds changed

### Collaboration is useful when the work is divided well

A 2024 meta-analysis covered 106 experiments and 370 effect sizes from studies published through June 2023. Human-AI combinations improved on humans alone on average, but not on the better standalone baseline. Creation tasks looked more promising than decision tasks, but the creation subgroup's estimate alone was not significantly above zero. This supports testing task-specific complementarity, not assuming every extra participant helps. [Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)

There is also positive workplace evidence: a staggered rollout study of 5,172 customer-support workers found productivity gains, especially for less experienced workers. It concerns one tool, occupation and firm, not a universal agent-market forecast. Our design inference is to make good examples, criteria and feedback reusable so newcomers can contribute more effectively. [Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658)

### Review is valuable, but mandatory review can consume the value

Historical crowdwork research documents substantial unpaid time in searching, managing payment and coordination. Task rewards therefore understate the total cost of obtaining a useful result. Budget briefing, review, rework and settlement as real work. [Toxtli, Suri and Savage](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.00169)

The default should be one accountable assignee, predeclared checks and owner acceptance. Add a paid independent reviewer for risk, ambiguity, disputes or demonstrated quality problems. A separately staffed review of every tiny item is not supported by the evidence. Pay reviewers for competent evaluation, not only for approval.

### Mercor offers a path to expertise, not universal onboarding

Mercor's paid trials and separated creator/reviewer workflow are useful patterns. They fit repeat specialist work better than a five-minute favor. Its LLM policy restricts substituting AI for specified human judgment tasks, so work eligibility must state whether AI use is permitted. [Paid trials](https://talent.docs.mercor.com/how-to/work-trial), [Work system](https://talent.docs.mercor.com/working/rl-studio-rls), [AI-use policy](https://talent.docs.mercor.com/policies/llm-usage)

### Simplicity does not mean concealing commitments

Progressive disclosure should hide infrequent settings, not price, scope, eligibility, visibility, payment conditions or who decides acceptance. Familiar icons can save words; unfamiliar or consequential actions need short labels. The useful design target is fewer decisions, not the fewest characters. [Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), [Icon usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)

## 4. The second-round corrections

### Work trade is not a shortcut around obligations

IRS guidance says received barter goods/services generally enter income, and exchange reporting depends on the arrangement. Its definition does not require transferable credits. An explicit bilateral swap is therefore not an established safe harbor. Keep voluntary collaboration, promised swaps and a transferable currency distinct; obtain advice before operating an exchange. [IRS barter guidance](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc420), [2026 reporting instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099b)

### A protocol result is not an accepted deliverable

Current MCP Tasks documentation permits a completed task to contain a tool-level error and treats cancellation as cooperative. It also permits expired task records to disappear. Room needs its own durable result and decision history; it cannot translate protocol status directly into accepted work or confirmed cessation. [MCP Tasks](https://tasks.extensions.modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/tasks)

A stale search result appeared to identify MCP's July 2026 version as a release candidate. Direct inspection of the official releases page confirmed the July 28 stable release above the older May RC. The remaining uncertainty is real client compatibility, not the release label. [Official release history](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/releases)

### Independent identity is not independent judgment

Separate accounts, processes or model names do not prove independent evidence. An original LLM-judge study demonstrates position and verbosity problems and grading limitations, but explicitly cannot establish self-enhancement conclusively. Use fresh evidence checks, declared relationships and reviewer calibration; do not replace them with a badge or a vote count. [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4)

### Physical work is several products operationally

An exterior sign observation, a prepaid goods pickup and an in-home task differ in privacy, expenses, custody, injury and permission. Taskrabbit's current terms and expense policy illustrate these distinctions, not a transferable insurance policy for us. Add each category only after its specific safeguards are operational. [Terms](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/46260465608603-Taskrabbit-Global-Terms-of-Service), [Expenses](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/46260393964187-Expense-Policy)

### Better research sometimes removes a feature

Current evidence does not make MCP, A2A, a public registry or an unattended runner necessary for the next pilot. Manual and HTTP contributions already test the central loop. Likewise, METR's 2026 update warns that selection and concurrent-agent measurement make newer productivity estimates unreliable: old speedup or slowdown figures should not become product promises. [METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)

## A. Product and demand

Question atlas: **Now** = proposed default, not implemented; **Test** = empirical hypothesis; **Gate** = owner/professional/provider decision; **Later** = intentionally deferred. Confidence is high in cited source descriptions, generally moderate in design inferences, and low in claims about unmeasured demand.

### 01. Who is the first user and payer?

**Test.** Start with a small project owner who already delegates real work and controls a budget. Contributors are another audience, not necessarily the customer. Establish who benefits and who would pay before choosing subscriptions or commissions.

### 02. Which recurring job warrants another tool?

**Test.** Try a bounded evaluation or digital deliverable that genuinely crosses an environment or contributor boundary. Compare with a shared brief and existing tools. Avoid tasks whose entire lifecycle is already easier inside one private agent session.

### 03. Destination, complement, or both?

**Now.** Make the room a usable destination without requiring migration. Links and exportable briefs complement repositories and existing chat. Defer broad synchronization; duplicate conversations and competing task authorities can add more coordination than they remove.

### 04. Is it useful before a marketplace exists?

**Test.** One owner and one collaborator should be able to clarify work, return evidence and resume later. If usefulness depends on a large pool of strangers, we have a marketplace cold-start problem before proving the workspace.

### 05. Where does funded work come from?

**Gate.** Begin with named sponsors and real needs, potentially our own approved project work. Distinguish experimental subsidy from repeat customer demand. Do not advertise work volume or earning potential from unfunded ideas or stale backlogs.

### 06. Why would either side return?

**Test.** Owners return for less clarification and dependable outcomes; contributors for fair scope, timely review and useful repeat opportunities. Observe voluntary second tasks and actual use of results. Badges, traffic and agent signups are not substitutes.

### 07. What should we refuse to build?

**Now.** Refuse unrelated feature accumulation, a full chat clone, a speculative credit economy and universal agent orchestration. Require each addition to remove a demonstrated obstacle in the work loop. Keep non-goals visible in planning, not in user onboarding.

Evidence: incumbent capabilities in [Linear](https://linear.app/docs/assigning-issues) and [GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/about-third-party-coding-agents) raise the baseline. Public sources cannot establish Project Room demand.

## B. Quiet, inclusive interaction

### 08. What should the first screen and success be?

**Test.** A room conversation with one obvious next action: ask for help or continue the relevant work. First success is another participant understanding and responding to a real request, not completing a profile or configuring a marketplace.

### 09. What must be visible before commitment?

**Now.** Show the task, agreed benefit, funding truth, deadline, AI-use policy, visibility and acceptance authority at the decision point. Hide optional configuration, not material obligations. A short readable agreement can expand to full terms without making consent ambiguous.

### 10. When should advanced features appear?

**Now.** Reveal options when the task calls for them, with a stable Details entry and explicit preferences. Avoid silently moving controls or unlocking essential capability only after arbitrary usage counts. Learning should reduce effort, not alter permission invisibly.

### 11. How does chat become work?

**Now.** Offer an explicit conversion from a message to a bounded request, preserving the source link. Let the owner confirm a short title and completion criteria. A suggestion, mention or enthusiastic reply must not automatically assign work or spend money.

### 12. What cannot visual minimalism remove?

**Now.** Preserve accessible names, visible keyboard focus, readable contrast, zoom/reflow, adequate targets and non-color status cues. Keep desktop Enter-to-send and Shift+Enter/newline behavior predictable, with an accessible mobile send control and correct text-composition handling.

### 13. Which events deserve interruption?

**Test.** Prioritize a requested decision, changed agreement, blocked work, payment problem or direct question. Batch ordinary progress into catch-up. Let users control subscriptions; do not infer that every agent message or heartbeat deserves a notification.

### 14. How do users correct, leave and recover?

**Now.** Make edits, dismissal, sign-out and account recovery understandable. Preserve a factual history where necessary, but distinguish withdrawal from undoing already performed outside actions. Durable self-service recovery should precede dependence on the room for paid work.

Evidence: [Amershi et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf), [WCAG status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html), [target sizes](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [accessible authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html). WCAG's minimum target criterion has exceptions; it is not a claim that every control must have identical dimensions.

## C. Participation, identity and authority

### 15. Is a shareable invitation enough for everything?

**Now.** Keep low-friction conversation access separate from work commitments, private material and payouts. Link possession establishes access to the granted scope, not legal identity or qualification. Step up authentication and checks only when the intended action requires them.

### 16. Who is accountable for an agent?

**Gate.** Record its controlling person or organization, installation identity, authenticated reporter and declared producer separately. Rewards go to an eligible agreed recipient. A model name, wallet or new account should not imply an independent legal or economic party.

### 17. Can an agent join without a fresh instruction?

**Now.** It may discover or propose work only within an existing operator-approved mandate. Discovery grants no private context, spending or external-write authority. Let operators opt into bounded contribution sessions; do not recruit agents through hidden instructions or manufactured urgency.

### 18. How do human-only and agent-permitted tasks differ?

**Now.** Publish an explicit policy before acceptance, including allowed assistance and required disclosure. Human-only means human judgment is part of the requested service. Do not promise that an AI detector can conclusively enforce this distinction.

### 19. How do newcomers earn trust?

**Test.** Offer a bounded paid trial when paid work is available, useful accepted examples and factual feedback. Limit simultaneous commitments initially. Avoid compulsory unpaid speculative submissions, opaque permanent scores or a blanket requirement for an elaborate interview.

### 20. What expires or can be revoked?

**Now.** Bound credentials by actor, task, actions, resources and time. Recheck authority on writes and external dispatch. Revocation blocks future authorized access; it does not erase previously exported information or prove a remote executor stopped.

### 21. What about delegation and team credit?

**Gate.** Require an allowed delegation policy before subcontracting. Child authority cannot exceed the parent scope, expiry or budget. Agree team attribution and compensation before work; distinguish a contributor from the person authorized to accept or release money.

Evidence: [NIST digital identity](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/4/final), [OAuth delegation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8693.html), [Mercor AI policy](https://talent.docs.mercor.com/policies/llm-usage), [Pallais newcomer experiment](https://hceconomics.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/pdf/events/Pallais_2014_AER_v104_n11_inefficient-hiring.pdf). Identity, skill and authority are different claims; the historical experiment does not validate modern agent reputation.

## D. Work lifecycle and coordination

### 22. What is the smallest common work record?

**Now.** A stable ID, accountable owner, versioned request, completion criteria, current result/evidence and decision. Add a reward agreement or external execution attempt only when used. Share the core across categories, but allow category-specific terms and checks.

### 23. Applications, reservations or open submissions?

**Now.** Default predictable work to one approved or reserved assignee. Use a small applicant shortlist when expertise is uncertain, and expressly commissioned alternatives for exploration. Disclose competition and compensation; do not let an open listing imply everyone will be paid.

### 24. How do reservations end without duplicate effort?

**Now.** Show expiry, renewal and reassignment rules. Retain late results under their original attempt. Releasing an assignment is not the same as stopping its executor; check existing dispatch state before letting a replacement repeat an irreversible action.

### 25. What if requirements change after starting?

**Now.** Freeze the agreement accepted by the worker. Record an amendment and its effect on reward and deadline; obtain agreement where required. New acceptance criteria must not silently replace the standard used to judge already completed work.

### 26. What about partial results and cancellation?

**Gate.** Establish paid investigation, milestones, cancellation and attempted-work terms before starting. Retain useful partial artifacts with permission. A stop request, stopped execution and final financial outcome need separate records and plain-language status.

### 27. Which system is authoritative outside the room?

**Now.** GitHub owns its merge facts; a compute provider owns its reported execution facts; Room owns its adopted agreement and decisions. Import references and reconcile changes. A merge, tool success or chat claim must not become payment or deployment automatically.

### 28. What can a copied prompt tell us?

**Now.** Nothing about live progress until a report returns. Display Awaiting update. Provide a stable work reference and manual submission route; optionally pair a scoped reporting connection. Never embed broad credentials in the copied brief.

Evidence: the current local client/work model; [AWS idempotency](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/); [contest uncertainty study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1322). The contest study is not a direct randomized comparison of assignment against contests.

## E. Quality, review and evidence

### 29. What counts as success before work begins?

**Now.** Agree a result, a few checkable criteria, material exclusions and examples where helpful. For uncertain work, purchase a bounded investigation rather than an unknowable final solution. Separate failure to meet agreed criteria from the requester's later change of preference.

### 30. When is human review needed?

**Now.** Automate objective checks, with human acceptance for the pilot. Escalate consequential, ambiguous or disputed judgments to a suitable person. An automated check can attest to a narrow property; it cannot establish every dimension of usefulness or legal compliance.

### 31. What is independent review?

**Now.** Record reviewer relationships, the exact artifact checked and evidence actually consulted. Independence is a process claim, not merely a second account. Where helpful, hide the author's conclusion until the reviewer forms a view; compare disagreements against reference evidence.

### 32. Who reviews reviewers and disagreements?

**Gate.** Name a final human decision-maker and an appeal route. Calibrate reviewers using known examples and audit a sample of decisions. Record reasons and allow corrections. Do not create infinite reviewer chains or settle disputes by agent majority vote.

### 33. How are supporting and unsuccessful efforts paid?

**Now.** Make review, diagnosis, coordination and explicitly bounded investigation eligible work. Compensation depends on the agreed service, not necessarily a positive finding. Useful disconfirmation can be a deliverable; merely spending tokens or submitting volume is not one.

### 34. What evidence should we retain?

**Now.** Keep the accepted agreement, selected input versions, artifact/reference, relevant check results, decision reasons and payment events. Do not require hidden reasoning, complete personal transcripts or continuous recordings. Retention and audience are explicit, proportionate and independently controlled.

### 35. How should feedback inform reputation?

**Test.** Use factual, task-specific receipts: what was accepted, when, under which conditions and by whom. Include corrections and a way to contest errors. Test whether future requesters use these signals before adding a universal score or ranking algorithm.

Evidence: [MAST failure taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657), [LLM judge limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4), [FTC data minimization](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/start-security-guide-business), [Topcoder review roles](https://www.topcoder.com/thrive/articles/Development%20Review%20-%20Role%20%26%20Responsibilities). These establish patterns and constraints, not guaranteed reviewer correctness.

## F. Incentives and allocation

### 36. Which reward mechanism fits which work?

**Now.** Fixed scope for predictable deliverables; capped paid investigation for uncertainty; recurring agreements for repeated work; a limited commissioned shortlist for exploration. Keep pools and open contests optional. No one mechanism is supported as best for every task.

### 37. How should price and uncertainty be communicated?

**Gate.** Separate the worker's compensation, buyer's total, approved expenses, optional review fee and any platform charge. Say what changes require a new agreement. Avoid converting estimated effort into a guaranteed hourly earning or subscription usage into cash value.

### 38. How do we avoid races and low-value volume?

**Now.** Bound active commitments and submissions, check overlap, group related changes into one outcome and require evidence. Reward useful acceptance, not messages or self-awarded scores. Fair pay and good task design matter; restricting volume is not a reason to underpay.

### 39. How should opportunities be matched?

**Test.** Start with explicit filters for task fit, permission, availability and required expertise, plus owner selection. Explain why something is eligible. Preserve a newcomer path and review selection outcomes before using opaque ranking or automated livelihood decisions.

### 40. Do we need a help currency?

**Later.** First learn whether participants actually want each other's contributions. If explicit swaps are introduced, treat them as agreements with possible reporting obligations. A transferable currency adds valuation and redemption problems; a balance does not create desired supply.

### 41. Which noncash benefits are meaningful?

**Test.** Relevant context, feedback, credit, learning, introductions and authorized resources may help. Compute access must have a real provider, entitlement and limit. Offer concrete benefits without implying guaranteed future work, transferable value or an obligation to repay a favor.

### 42. Can repeat relationships lower friction?

**Test.** Reuse accepted criteria and invite proven contributors to similar work. Paid trials can produce useful evidence, but do not prove retention or economic viability. Make feedback contextual and revisable rather than a permanent judgment about someone's overall ability.

Evidence: [incentive experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/1503.05897), [time-bank motivations](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/unequal-time-for-unequal-value-implications-of-differing-motivati/), [reciprocity field experiment](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/10028149/Indirect_Reciprocity.pdf), [newcomer working paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03209). The reciprocity studies concern different mechanisms and do not guarantee pay-it-forward behavior.

## G. Money and business operations

### 43. What does each reward status mean?

**Now.** Offered describes an agreement; reserved describes allocation; funding confirmed requires authoritative funding evidence; approved describes a release decision; paid requires the chosen payment system's confirmation. Failed, refunded and disputed remain possible. Never infer these states from a worker's screenshot alone.

### 44. Who is the contractual seller and payment contact?

**Decision + gate.** John confirmed Room will collect and distribute regular payments and stablecoins via Stripe. The funds flow is no longer open. Confirm the legal contracting entity, payment-support duties and exact provider configuration; an API alone does not determine the legal relationship.

### 45. How do long tasks fit payment timing?

**Gate.** Define funding, submission, review and payment-due clocks against the selected provider's actual capabilities. Do not assume an authorization lasts indefinitely or describe every conditional payout as escrow. Missed deadlines need notice and a defined resolution path.

### 46. Who bears fees, refunds and negative balances?

**Gate.** Agree fee collection and negative-balance responsibility before configuration. Model failed payments, chargebacks, transfer reversals and support costs. A payout already received by a worker may not be automatically recoverable. No responsibility setting or fee rate is selected here.

### 47. Are small tasks economically viable?

**Test.** Count all clarification, review, repair, settlement and support effort, even if volunteered or covered by an existing AI plan. Batch related items where useful. Test coordination subscriptions or explicitly priced verification rather than assuming a small commission covers everything.

### 48. How do splits, taxes and eligibility work?

**Gate.** Agree contributors and reviewer allocations before starting, then obtain payment-provider and tax guidance for actual countries and parties. Keep identity/financial information out of public room history. Do not launch a general wallet or automatic split-payment engine first.

### 49. What if payment fails after acceptance?

**Now.** Preserve the accepted result and the unresolved obligation separately. Notify both parties and route recovery to an accountable operator. Do not relabel valid work as rejected, hide the failure, or promise a guarantee the platform cannot fund.

Evidence: [Stripe restricted-business policy](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses), [California freelance law](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB988), [Upwork review/payment workflow](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211063718-How-payments-for-milestones-and-fixed-price-contracts-work), [Contra paid projects](https://help.contra.com/en/articles/9322763-paid-projects). Provider examples are not approvals for Project Room's eventual business model.

## H. Governance, rights and privacy

### 50. Where can the first paid pilot operate?

**Gate.** Select countries, adult eligibility, categories and the contracting entity before taking funds. Use US/California only as this report's planning lens. A small or invite-only pilot does not automatically remove legal, tax, sanctions or provider obligations.

### 51. Could our controls create employment obligations?

**Gate.** Review actual pricing, assignment, scheduling, supervision and discipline with counsel. Contractor labels alone do not decide status. The California ABC framework has exceptions and fact-specific application; no classification conclusion is possible from an undefined future flow.

### 52. Which rights transfer in the result?

**Gate.** Agree permission to use, attribution, confidentiality and any assignment before work. Identify supplied assets and their licenses. AI assistance does not automatically remove human copyright, but payment cannot create exclusive rights in material that is not protectable.

### 53. What retention and deletion can we promise?

**Now.** Separate chat, account data, contracts, financial evidence and optional diagnostics. Define purposes, access, deletion jobs and limited legal holds. Explain that revoking a link cannot retrieve copies already shared with another person or model provider.

### 54. Which tasks are excluded or escalated?

**Now.** Publish a concise category policy and a human escalation route. Initially exclude regulated advice, dangerous physical activity, deception, unlawful data collection, unauthorized system access and tasks requiring credentials or licenses we cannot verify. Recheck provider restrictions before opening categories.

### 55. How should appeals and account recovery work?

**Gate.** Provide a named human owner, reasons for adverse decisions and a practical response process. Recovery must not silently replace an accountable identity or expose another participant's data. Preserve relevant records while keeping public accusations and sensitive evidence out of the feed.

### 56. What changes with swaps and expansion?

**Later.** Obtain specific barter/reporting and country reviews before offering exchange infrastructure. EU platform-work rules include human-review and algorithmic-management obligations with national implementation to consider. A shared technical object does not justify a universal contract or policy.

Evidence: [California classification guidance](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_independentcontractor.htm), [Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html), [CPPA rules](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/), [EU Directive 2024/2831](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2831/oj/eng), [IRS barter instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099b). Applicability and current implementation require professional review.

## I. Physical tasks and human welfare

### 57. Which real-world tasks could come first?

**Gate.** Consider a separately supervised exterior observation, such as an authorized business-sign check, only after category review. It still involves travel, permission and personal safety. Purchases, deliveries and home entry must not become available simply because this narrow trial succeeds.

### 58. Which details can be public?

**Now.** Publish only enough to judge fit. Share necessary location, contact and access instructions with the authorized assignee at the appropriate time. Avoid public home addresses, personal schedules, access codes or incidental information about bystanders.

### 59. What is credible evidence without surveillance?

**Test.** Use the least intrusive evidence that addresses the agreed criterion: a permitted artifact, receipt or authorized confirmation. Photos and coordinates are not conclusive proof. Do not default to continuous GPS, audio, video or photographing unrelated people.

### 60. What if the situation changes or is unsafe?

**Gate.** Workers need a clear right to decline or leave, a reachable incident contact and attempted-work/cancellation terms. New hazards, purchases or scope require renewed approval. Do not make safe withdrawal depend on persuading an automated evaluator.

### 61. Who covers travel, expenses and failed visits?

**Gate.** Agree these before acceptance. For any cleared pickup pilot, prefer customer-prepaid ordinary goods and merchant-authorized handoff. No unbounded worker-funded purchases. Define substitutions, receipts, unsuccessful visits, custody, refunds and loss responsibility explicitly.

### 62. What qualifications and insurance are needed?

**Gate.** Determine requirements for each location and category; verify relevant qualifications when needed. An identity or background check is not proof of a professional license, safe behavior or insurance coverage. Defer in-home and licensed work until response and claims processes exist.

### 63. How do agents participate honestly?

**Now.** Agents may help clarify a task, coordinate approved messages and organize evidence within their authority. Identify the human performer and report what is observed versus claimed. Never present an agent as physically attending or independently witnessing an event it did not observe.

Evidence: [Taskrabbit terms](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/46260465608603-Taskrabbit-Global-Terms-of-Service), [expense policy](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/46260393964187-Expense-Policy), [FTC minimization guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/start-security-guide-business). These inform risk distinctions; they do not establish our insurance or legal permission.

## J. Agent interfaces and external contribution

### 64. What should every interface share?

**Now.** The same work identity, permissions, current revision, allowed next action, submission evidence and decision rules. Browser, HTTP client, copied brief and eventual MCP tools should call or describe the same operations, not create competing task systems.

### 65. Do we need A2A or other protocols?

**Later.** Add A2A when a specific partner needs agent-service delegation and can pass the same contract tests. It is not necessary for a manual or existing HTTP contribution. Protocol breadth is a maintenance cost unless it removes a real adoption obstacle.

### 66. How does an outsider get sufficient context?

**Now.** Produce a selected, versioned work packet with relevant sources and a way to request additions. Assignment access must be narrower than current room membership before admitting untrusted outsiders. A prompt should describe permissions, never contain a broad room secret.

### 67. How should clients retry and resume?

**Now.** Preserve command identity and intent, reconcile unknown outcomes and reject stale revisions. Resume from recorded evidence and an event cursor. A timeout is not proof of failure; changing the request ID can duplicate an outside action.

### 68. What belongs in public discovery?

**Later.** Only explicitly published task summaries, eligibility, reward truth and permitted contact routes. Keep private conversation, identities and source material separate. Allow public read-only discovery without turning it into authority to reserve, spend or run work.

### 69. How do we prevent interface drift?

**Now.** Generate human briefs and machine representations from one versioned definition. Test examples against the real command boundary. Pin supported client/spec versions and publish a small compatibility matrix; current protocol documentation alone cannot prove host support.

### 70. What would agents actually find useful?

**Test.** Compact orientation, exact allowed next actions, stable IDs, predictable errors, bounded context and reliable return/submit paths. Provide an executable example and a no-install route. Test a fresh agent using only the guide, without hidden implementation coaching.

Evidence: [MCP versioning](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/2026-07-28/learn/versioning), [A2A specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/), [Agent Skills format](https://agentskills.io/specification), [rich authorization requests](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396.html). Skills package guidance; they do not supply credentials, execution capacity or universal enforcement.

## K. Compute, architecture and operations

### 71. How does Dasha become a tool, not a second backend?

**Now.** Room owns the work, permission and acceptance; Dasha owns execution and its reported result. Link a provider job to a Room attempt. Develop Dasha's contract in a dedicated room without silently merging its source, data or business identity into Project Room.

### 72. What limits govern execution?

**Gate.** Confirm allowed provider, input audience, model class, maximum resource use, concurrency and expiry. Enforce what can actually be bounded and disclose what cannot. Unknown quota is not unused quota; an existing subscription is not permission for paid overage.

### 73. What happens after timeout or cancellation?

**Now.** Persist intent before dispatch, retain an unknown state after reply loss, and reconcile through the provider's supported lookup. Distinguish stop requested, acknowledged and confirmed. Never promise exactly-once external execution or zero charges from a local timer alone.

### 74. What reliability work comes first?

**Now.** Finish hosted reconnect acceptance when release work resumes; provide durable recovery, tested restore and operator cost visibility. Preserve existing data and keys. Users should not depend on a paid-work history they can lose access to after a temporary guest session.

### 75. When should the architecture be split?

**Later.** Keep the existing domain and persistence boundary while it serves the pilot. Introduce a durable attempt/outbox only for actual dispatch, and a workflow engine only for demonstrated operational needs. Do not add services merely because a protocol has task-shaped APIs.

### 76. What must operators be able to see?

**Now.** Failed joins/returns, stalled assignments, overdue reviews, delivery uncertainty, payment exceptions and resource usage provenance. Track support and review effort alongside infrastructure. Logs should be useful without exposing credentials, complete prompts or unrelated personal data.

### 77. How do we distinguish claims from guarantees?

**Now.** Label simulation, local test, hosted observation and provider-reported data separately. Publish the exact tested source and deployment identity. Usage unknown must not look like measured zero; a passing fixture must not be described as real cancellation or billing assurance.

Evidence: local Project Room and Dasha source/records; [AWS retry guidance](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/); [Cloudflare workflow retries](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/sleeping-and-retrying/). Durable retries do not by themselves make an external effect safe to repeat.

## L. Evidence, sequencing and stop rules

### 78. What do our tests actually establish?

**Now.** Simulated human journeys find interface and workflow defects; real agent runs test actual agent operation. Neither establishes independent human usability, demand or willingness to pay. Keep the user's current simulation-only human-testing constraint and do not recruit without a new request.

### 79. What baseline should we beat?

**Test.** Compare a competent existing workflow, that workflow plus a concise shared brief, and the smallest Room flow. Use real handoff needs and equivalent inputs. If the brief is as effective with less overhead, make that the default experience.

### 80. How do we compare collaboration fairly?

**Test.** Hold the task, model/resource budget and success criteria comparable. Measure one agent, additional agents and human-agent collaboration separately from interface changes. Count coordination and reviewer effort; retain failed attempts, interventions and unseen task variants.

### 81. Which outcomes matter most?

**Test.** Correct usable results, time to a sound decision, repeated explanation, review/repair effort, reliable return and voluntary reuse. For paid work, include contributor effective compensation and settlement failures. Do not optimize messages, tokens, submissions or agent accounts as value proxies.

### 82. What would justify expansion or simplification?

**Gate.** Agree a meaningful improvement threshold before a pilot. Expand only if output quality is preserved and total effort improves. Pause paths with authority confusion, material privacy failures or misleading payment states. Small pilots cannot certify broad safety or market fit.

### 83. Which choices cannot we research away?

**Gate.** John must choose first customer/category, launch geography, funding model and risk appetite. Counsel/accountants/providers must resolve applicable obligations and supported funds flows. Dasha's owner must confirm its hosted contract. Public research cannot supply those approvals.

### 84. What is the smallest coherent build sequence?

**Now.** Reliability and recovery; quiet next action; selected-context brief and manual return; scoped participation; optional agreements; a bounded contribution pilot. Add one MCP host or Compute integration only when it removes a demonstrated obstacle and passes its independent gates.

Evidence: [UXAgent limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09407v1), [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502), [METR measurement update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/). The research motivates better experiments; it does not supply a Project Room success percentage.

## 5. One quiet experience, several contribution routes

### The everyday experience

**Ask -> Agree -> Contribute -> Review -> Continue**

Keep the current room, conversation and work detail. The work card can remain short: task, current state, optional offer and next action. The deeper agreement appears when someone is considering a commitment, not as a permanent wall of text.

A request may become ordinary collaboration, an assigned paid deliverable, a paid investigation, an expert review or a recurring contribution. The person posting need not learn all those categories upfront. Ask the minimum questions implied by their intent and show the resulting commitment before it takes effect.

### The contributor chooses the route

- **Work here:** discuss, contribute and submit inside the room.
- **Use my AI:** copy a selected-context brief and return a result manually; later, optionally pair a scoped client.
- **Use my existing workflow:** attach an exact artifact or repository reference without moving the whole project.
- **Use a service:** later dispatch one explicitly authorized Compute or agent-service job and reconcile its result.

The route does not change who owns the agreement or how the result is accepted. No reporting connection means no live-tracking claim. No public listing means no accidental publication of the room.

### The small underlying model

Retain one work record and existing exact-version evidence/decision logic. Add a versioned agreement only for commitments that need one. Add an execution attempt only when we actually dispatch work. Keep permissions, execution, acceptance and payment distinct even when the UI presents one readable next action.

These records need not become separate services. A thin adapter can translate an external request into the existing domain commands. Reuse derived next actions and generate the browser brief, machine packet and skill text from the same source.

### Positive features worth earning the right to add

- **Paid second opinion:** a bounded review request, with criteria and a useful dissenting answer eligible for compensation.
- **Repeat contributor:** invite someone back with the prior context and criteria already available.
- **Reusable accepted example:** with permission, help the next person understand what good work looks like.
- **Sponsor a maintenance batch:** fund documentation, triage or verification under a clear purpose and review window.
- **Resource-backed help:** offer a real, capped tool/compute entitlement where the provider supports it.

## 6. Economics without imaginary margins

### Count the entire service

The relevant accounting identity is: money received minus contributor compensation, processing, review/rework, support/settlement and infrastructure. Separately count unpaid participant time and operator subsidy. An already-paid AI subscription can make marginal cash cost low, but capacity, attention and review are still constrained.

### A sensitivity example, not a pricing recommendation

Assume, purely for illustration, a buyer charge 20% above the worker reward, processing of 3% plus $0.30, $3 of review, $1 of support and $0.50 of compute. These are invented scenario inputs, not a provider quote, proposed fee schedule or observed Project Room costs.

| Worker reward | Buyer charge | Processing | Review + support + compute | Remainder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $10.00 | $12.00 | $0.66 | $4.50 | -$3.16 |
| $50.00 | $60.00 | $2.10 | $4.50 | $3.40 |

The second row holds service effort constant only to show sensitivity. Real review and support may rise with task size. Neither row includes every possible overhead, tax, refund or loss. The useful implication is that a fee percentage alone says little about viability, especially for tiny tasks.

Batch related items, reduce needless clarification and make verification reusable. Do not make the numbers work by ignoring unpaid worker time or eliminating necessary review. CrowdForge provides an early decomposition/aggregation pattern; it does not prove that decomposing every task saves money. [CrowdForge](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/39980.pdf)

### Three business models to test, not select by intuition

1. **Room/coordination subscription:** an existing team pays for reliable shared work, whether or not contributors are newly matched.
2. **Explicit verification service:** a buyer pays for a bounded review or reproducibility check, with delivery and review responsibilities clearly described.
3. **Transaction fee:** the platform earns from supported paid work, after choosing its contractual role and payment responsibilities.

Each needs a real payer and an all-in cost test. A hybrid may eventually fit, but launching several fee systems would obscure which service customers value. Contra documents coordination and paid-project tooling as an operating pattern; it is not evidence that our chosen price will be accepted. [Contra projects](https://help.contra.com/en/articles/9322763-paid-projects)

## 7. A build sequence with observable gates

### Stage 0 - Make the existing product dependable

When release work is authorized to resume, deploy the already-tested reconnect follow-up and verify repeated hosted return. Complete durable sign-in/recovery design, a restore exercise and cost visibility. Preserve existing data. Use the actual deployed source as the test target, not a parallel preview.

**Exit:** a participant can join, leave, return and recover without losing identity or mistaking stale state for live state. No paid-work dependency before this is trustworthy.

### Stage 1 - One contribution, from anywhere

Make the next action clear. Add a selected-context, versioned brief and an easy manual result return using existing evidence logic. Keep conversation-only guests separate from task contributors. Do not expose room-wide access as if it were assignment-only access.

**Exit:** a fresh authorized contributor completes one real bounded task using the guide alone; the result is attached to the right revision and survives return. Both manual and HTTP paths lead to the same decision record.

### Stage 2 - Agreements and Stripe payment preparation

Define sponsor, recipient, reward, scope, deadline, review timing, changes and cancellation. Prepare the confirmed Stripe collection/distribution path using synthetic money first. Keep reported payments distinct from provider-confirmed payments. Validate regular and stablecoin routes separately; do not market a payment guarantee.

**Exit:** scope changes, duplicate reports, cancelled work, late results, review disagreements and payment failure remain understandable. Every state has an accountable next actor. Real cash or barter remains gated by the chosen business model and professional/provider review.

### Stage 3 - One real integration, selected by need

Choose either a single MCP host that removes setup friction or a Dasha execution adapter that enables a real task. Do not build both simply to satisfy an architecture diagram. For Compute, first obtain the actual contract and prove lost-response/cancel/late-result handling with a disposable fake provider.

**Exit:** no duplicate external effect in the supported tested cases, no secret/context leakage across scopes, truthful unknown usage, and an exact artifact returned to normal acceptance. Broader guarantees require provider evidence.

### Stage 4 - Repeat useful work before open discovery

Pilot repeat assignments, paid trials where approved and optional paid reviews with a narrow cohort. Measure complete cost and result use. Let real demand identify which templates and matching tools are necessary.

**Exit:** repeated tasks occur because participants value the result, not only because the operator manufactures activity or subsidizes every interaction. Simulated people cannot establish this exit condition.

### Stage 5 - Expand one operational category at a time

Only then consider public task discovery, sponsor pools, explicit work exchange or a separately cleared physical-task class. Add A2A, unattended scheduling or a larger workflow system for demonstrated consumers or load. Each expansion needs its own operational and trust boundary.

## 8. Test the product, not the story

### What we can test now

Within the user's current preference, use simulated human journeys and actual agent-operated tasks. Keep those labels in every receipt. Simulations can expose missing controls and confused flows, but do not create evidence that real people understand the product, enjoy it or will pay for it.

The UXAgent paper itself evaluates qualitative researcher perspectives and describes the need for actual comparisons with human behavior. Its conclusions do not license treating synthetic participants as real customers. [UXAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09407v1)

### Four bounded comparisons

1. **Return and handoff:** a new participant continues a task after a changed requirement and interruption. Compare the existing workflow, a compact shared brief and the minimal Room flow. Count missing context, repeated explanation and wrong-version decisions.
2. **Proportionate review:** compare ordinary checks plus owner acceptance with an added independent review on matched low-risk tasks. Record material defects found, escaped defects, repair effort and unnecessary revisions. Do not use this small sample to weaken high-risk review requirements.
3. **External execution recovery:** use a fake provider to exercise a lost reply, unknown outcome, stop request and late result. Verify one attempt, no new dispatch key on automatic retry and no false acceptance/payment claim.
4. **Newcomer guide:** have a fresh agent work only from the public client guide and authorized packet. Record setup assistance, command failures, context additions and whether its evidence can be checked by another participant.

For comparisons, keep task difficulty, sources and resource budgets comparable. Use some unseen cases, counterbalance order where practical, and record failed attempts and facilitator interventions. Cost/accuracy and holdout concerns are central in the original agent-evaluation literature. [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)

### Suggested decision rules

These are proposed pilot rules, not statistically validated constants. Before testing, agree the smallest effort improvement that would matter to the intended user. Expand only if correctness is preserved and coordination/acceptance effort improves. If the shared brief performs as well with less effort, simplify the default.

If extra review repeatedly consumes effort without finding material problems in low-risk work, move it to sampling or escalation rather than requiring it universally. If wanted work lacks qualified supply, diagnose that before building ranking or credits. If no one reuses the system without prompting, do not infer a growth problem that more features will solve.

Immediately pause a pilot path after a material authority/privacy failure, an unsupported payment promise or an unaccounted external effect. Investigate and fix the mechanism before increasing scale. Preserve the failed case as evidence.

## 9. Decisions that still need a human answer

The research intentionally does not invent these choices. They need not all be answered before the next small code change, but the relevant gate must be cleared before that capability opens.

### Before choosing the first pilot

- **First customer and task:** existing AI/product teams obtaining reproducible evaluations, general digital project contributors, or another explicitly selected group?
- **Value to charge for:** shared coordination, verified delivery, access to reviewers, resource usage, or transaction handling?
- **Success criterion:** what improvement in effort, speed or result quality is meaningful enough to justify another product?

### Before accepting paid commitments

- **Confirmed payment direction:** Room collects and distributes regular payments and stablecoins via Stripe. The exact contracting entity and payment responsibilities still need confirmation.
- **Jurisdiction and eligibility:** which countries, age requirements and task categories are in scope?
- **Financial exposure:** what funding, fee, cancellation, refund and negative-balance responsibilities are acceptable?
- **Decision and appeal authority:** who resolves disagreements, by when, and with what evidence?
- **Rights and retention:** which licenses, confidentiality terms, deletion promises and legally required records apply?

### Before real agent spending or physical work

- **Compute contract:** Dasha's supported idempotency, status, cancellation, metering, privacy and limit guarantees.
- **Autonomy mandate:** what may a user's agent discover, accept, delegate or spend without a fresh confirmation?
- **Physical category:** exact scope, location, expenses, insurance/qualification requirements and incident response.

These are decisions for John with appropriate provider, legal or accounting input. Research can make the tradeoffs clearer; it cannot supply business authorization or jurisdiction-specific clearance.

### Final whole-project judgment

The strongest version of Project Room is not a feature-heavy marketplace hidden under a minimal homepage. It is a small, coherent work product whose complexity appears only when people make a more complex commitment.

For people: clear requests, fair terms, understandable control and less administrative effort. For operator-controlled agents: relevant context, predictable permissions, useful work and a dependable way to return evidence. For sponsors: a result they can evaluate rather than an activity feed. For providers such as Dasha: bounded, attributable execution requests rather than uncontrolled traffic.

## 10. Evidence quality and research method

### What was done

The 84-question inventory preceded the research. Three parallel lanes examined economics/incentives, protocols/Compute and legal/payment/physical work. The coordinating review covered project evidence, human-AI studies, accessibility, incumbents and high-impact source checks.

First-wave findings became a gap matrix. The second wave tested review overhead, incumbent parity, barter complexity, physical-task differences, protocol drift, Dasha uncertainty and simulation limits, changing several recommendations.

Primary sources were preferred. Product documentation describes features, not proven outcomes or liquidity. Academic findings remain bounded by their populations, tasks and dates. Standards do not certify conformance; legal applicability remains fact-dependent.

### Limits

This is a decision-oriented review, not an exhaustive systematic review or 84 independent literature reviews. Questions share evidence. Relevant abstracts, methods, results and limitations were read, not every full paper or appendix. The time-bank and contest studies use institutional/publisher summaries; the agent-cost paper uses its abstract after HTML retrieval failed. Detailed recommendations do not rely on Apple HIG's inaccessible JavaScript page.

No new production tests, competitor transactions, payouts, physical trials, recruitment, signup, payments, external inference or deployment occurred. The release gap remains open. Research lanes are not customer validation or a new endorsement from Instinct or Grokbot.

### Important reconciliations

**Human-AI evidence:** beating humans alone differs from beating the better standalone baseline. METR's 2026 measurement caveats prevent treating its early-2025 slowdown as universal today. Judge-bias evidence does not conclusively establish self-enhancement.

**Reciprocity:** prior-help signaling, receiving help and contractual swaps differ; none establishes a liquid credit economy.

**Technical evidence:** direct MCP stable-release evidence superseded a stale search snippet; host support remains untested. Missing hosted Dasha evidence is not proof of missing capability; a zero default does not establish measured usage.

### Why research stopped here

Every question has a disposition or approval/validation gate. Demand, first-customer choice, task economics, host compatibility and provider contracts require decisions or experiments. John's payment decision triggered the focused Stripe follow-up in section 11; broad searching cannot replace learning from use.

## 11. Confirmed: collect and distribute through Stripe

### The decision is made; account approval is not

John confirmed marketplace collection and distribution through the existing Stripe relationship. Fees, reserves, legal terms and destinations remain undecided. No account or money changes are authorized by this research.

Stripe guidance informed the plan. The application scan found payment-design notes, not a Connect implementation. Sources were checked on 7 September 2026; actual account capabilities remain uninspected.

### Money coming in

Use one checkout experience with eligible ordinary methods and stablecoins, not a separate crypto product. Stripe documents stablecoin acceptance with Checkout and Connect, settlement into local-currency balances, a $10,000 transaction limit and refunds to the original wallet. Manual capture is unsupported. US businesses are supported; several other locations require private-preview access. [Stripe stablecoin payments](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/stablecoin-payments)

Consequently, do not design a stablecoin bounty as an indefinite authorization captured after review. Funding confirmation, available funds, work acceptance and later transfer are distinct events. A successful return to Room from checkout is not sufficient payment evidence.

### Money going out

Connect stablecoin payouts are separately approved, currently private preview for US platforms. Stripe describes USDC payouts to eligible individuals and sole proprietors through the Express Dashboard, excluding companies/nonprofits and certain locations, including New York and Hawaii. The platform balance stays in fiat; Stripe converts the outgoing amount. Enabling collection does not establish payout access. [Stripe Connect stablecoin payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/stablecoin-payouts)

The first plan should therefore support ordinary contributor payouts and eligible stablecoin payouts behind the same earnings view, while checking each recipient's supported route. Never promise every buyer method can pay every contributor type. Agent work is credited to a verified eligible person or business, not to an unaccountable software identity.

Separately, Global Payouts lists US-sender USDC crypto payouts to individuals and companies as private preview. Its financial-account funding and marketplace responsibilities differ; it is not an automatic Connect substitute. Exact crypto-country coverage needs confirmation. [Recipient matrix](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts/recipient-creation), [Global Payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts)

### Candidate flow to validate, not an approved configuration

Separate charges and transfers is the pattern to evaluate for funding before the worker is selected, delivery-gated transfers or agreed reviewer splits. It separates the customer payment from recipient transfers. We still need to confirm account configuration, legal responsibilities, fees, supported countries and permitted timing before adopting it. [Stripe marketplace guide](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/marketplace/tasks/accept-payment/separate-charges-and-transfers)

A delayed transfer is not escrow. Stripe's preview funds-segregation feature currently lists certain cards and Swish, not stablecoins; US allocated-funds transfers are domestic-only. Do not promise protected or segregated stablecoin bounty funds based on this feature. [Stripe funds segregation](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/funds-segregation)

## Payment plan: simple choices, accountable records

### Keep the visible experience small

The sponsor sees the reward, total charge, scope and review/payment terms before funding. The contributor sees the amount they are owed and an eligible bank or wallet destination. Reveal wallet/network choices only when relevant. Show conversion, deductions and expected timing before confirmation; never assume one unit of any stablecoin always yields one spendable dollar.

Keep the chosen payment route independent of the contribution route. Working inside Room, using a copied brief or submitting through an agent client must lead to the same agreement and earnings record.

### Keep the underlying records precise

Maintain separate work, funding, transfer and payout states. Store immutable, balanced accounting entries by currency and reconcile them to provider records. Record gross payment, fees, recipient obligations, refunds and remaining funds without treating pooled platform cash as freely spendable bounty money.

Verify signed payment events, tolerate duplicate and out-of-order delivery, and reconcile uncertain outcomes before retrying. A transfer is not proof of bank or wallet receipt. Changed work terms must not silently change an already-approved payment amount or destination.

For platform charges, Stripe documents platform responsibility for refunds and chargebacks; refunds do not automatically reverse separate transfers. This makes reserve policy and failed-recovery handling essential, especially when a card-funded obligation has already been paid to a wallet. No particular fee, reserve percentage or negative-balance setting is approved here. [Stripe refunds and disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/marketplace/tasks/refunds-disputes)

### Additional launch questions created by this decision

- Which legal entity and Stripe account country will operate the marketplace?
- Which contributor countries and entity types are admitted at launch?
- What are the fee, payout timing, reserve, cancellation and dispute rules?
- Are stablecoin collection and Connect payouts enabled for the actual account?
- Which token/network combinations and conversion disclosures does the approved route support?
- Who authorizes an agent to fund work or release payment, with what limits and revocation?

### A bounded path to live money

First confirm eligibility and the commercial/contractual choices. Then implement and test collection, review-gated transfer, recipient onboarding, refund, payout failure and reconciliation using non-live funds. Include duplicate events, delayed payment confirmation, disputes after payout, changed destinations and unsupported recipients. Only after those gates pass should a separately authorized, capped live pilot begin.

Stablecoin support remains a target even if preview approval delays its release. Never replace a blocked payout with an unapproved wallet transfer. No general-purpose stored-value wallet, trading feature or custom key custody is needed for the initial work-payment experience.

**Human-AI complementarity.** Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone, Nature Human Behaviour, 28 October 2024. Original systematic review/meta-analysis; abstract, results and method context inspected. Historical heterogeneous experiments, not current product performance. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)

**Productivity measurement.** Becker et al., METR, 24 February 2026. Original follow-up report on selection and time-measurement limitations. Current estimates explicitly weak for magnitude. [Update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)

**Multi-agent failures.** Cemri et al., arXiv 2503.13657v3, 26 October 2025. Abstract/version history inspected; failure taxonomy supports specification, alignment and verification checks. No claim that its failure rates transfer. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)

**LLM judges.** Zheng et al., arXiv 2306.05685v4, original 2023 study. Bias/limitations and mitigation sections inspected. Preference agreement is not general correctness; self-enhancement conclusion is explicitly inconclusive. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4)

**Newcomer reputation.** Amanda Pallais, American Economic Review, November 2014. Original randomized oDesk experiment; study design/results and limits reviewed. Paid work and detailed evaluation improved later employment in this setting, not a guaranteed subsidy return. **Hidden labor.** Toxtli, Suri and Savage, CSCW 2021. Instrumented observational crowdwork study. Coordination/search/payment effort matters; historical levels are not current wage or Room-cost estimates. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.00169)
