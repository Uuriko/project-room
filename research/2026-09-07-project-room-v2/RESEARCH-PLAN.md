## New questions (48; coordination and automation added at John's request)

### A. Financial exposure across payment routes

N01. What exposure is created when reversible incoming payments finance already-completed stablecoin payouts?
N02. Should a bounty promise an amount in dollars, a token quantity or a recipient net amount, and who absorbs conversion differences?
N03. Can funds be accepted before a recipient is selected or payout-eligible without creating stranded obligations?
N04. How should limits apply across tasks, rooms and related identities rather than one transaction at a time?

### B. Failure, continuity and exit

N05. What happens to earned money and review obligations if a room is abandoned or the operator stops responding?
N06. What survives loss of payout eligibility, a closed account or an unusable wallet after work is accepted?
N07. Can participants leave the platform with useful agreements, artifacts and evidence without leaking another person's private information?
N08. Which exception states need a human operator before opening paid work, even at tiny scale?

### C. Evidence and shared understanding

N09. What does a result receipt prove: identity, integrity, provenance, reproduction, correctness or usefulness?
N10. How does a recipient know which context changed after a work packet or summary was issued?
N11. When does a handoff need explicit acknowledgment rather than mere message delivery?
N12. What if the acceptance criteria are measurable but reward the wrong outcome?

### D. Participation and reward design

N13. When could cash rewards weaken voluntary cooperation or change who contributes?
N14. Which preparation or investigation deserves payment even without a completed deliverable?
N15. Can transparent trial or reservation rules improve newcomer access without turning contribution into unpaid competition?
N16. Should requester reliability be visible alongside worker history, and how can feedback resist retaliation?

### E. Agent usefulness and resource allocation

N17. When does assigning another agent add independent information rather than correlated confidence?
N18. Can an agent help most by declining, asking a question or escalating instead of producing another artifact?
N19. Is unused compute actually the scarce input, or are good tasks, trustworthy data and review capacity scarcer?
N20. How should the system stop work that is still generating plausible output but no longer reducing uncertainty?

### F. Quiet interface under consequential actions

N21. Can stable, user-chosen disclosure outperform an interface that automatically changes as it infers expertise?
N22. Which moments need a concise pre-action preview, and when do confirmations merely train people to ignore them?
N23. How can the next action distinguish an ordinary message, a work commitment and a money-moving instruction without permanent visual noise?
N24. What information belongs in a resumable brief rather than the live chat stream?

### G. Demand and ecosystem boundaries

N25. Which first job has enough cross-tool friction to beat a well-written shared brief and existing project tools?
N26. Can a sponsor-facing preparation service create better work supply before a contributor marketplace exists?
N27. What keeps users on Room voluntarily after two parties know each other, without coercive lock-in?
N28. Which external source can be authoritative for the artifact while Room remains authoritative for agreement and acceptance?

### H. Learning and responsible scale

N29. How can a pilot separate orchestration benefit from better briefing, more model budget or operator help?
N30. What hidden failures does simulated human testing systematically miss?
N31. How should estimates and provisional confidence remain legible without turning a weak pilot into marketing claims?
N32. What must be removed or simplified if the first pilot succeeds technically but participants do not return?

### I. Avoiding collisions without stopping cooperation

N33. How do we detect overlapping objectives as well as concurrent edits to the same resource?
N34. What should be exclusively claimed, and what should remain open to parallel exploration or independent review?
N35. What does an expired claim mean when an agent may still be running elsewhere?
N36. Where must current ownership and expected revisions be enforced, rather than merely displayed?
N37. How are compatible pieces integrated without silently discarding either contributor's work?
N38. What happens when a retry might repeat an external action whose outcome is unknown?
N39. How can a person take over, interrupt or reassign work without causing a second active writer?
N40. How do we prove coordination prevents costly collisions without making small tasks slow and bureaucratic?

### J. User-owned agents, Room assistance, and a good free product

N41. Which useful automation needs no model, versus an external agent or a Room-hosted assistant?
N42. Can users choose their own agents without losing essential workflow or being charged twice without realizing it?
N43. Which first hosted assists remove real effort without making Room feel like another chatbot?
N44. What remains fully usable when the free AI allowance reaches zero?
N45. How do metering, free abuse limits, context cost and support cost determine a sustainable allowance?
N46. How should monthly paid plans and upgrade prompts add value without coercive interruption or surprise charges?
N47. Can deterministic rules, external agents and hosted agents share one ownership/approval system without loops or duplicate effects?
N48. What happens to automation, access, commitments and exports when an agent disconnects, a plan downgrades or the provider closes?
